A traditional Bengali winter treat

Make Bhapa Pitha at home.

A soft, gently crumbly steamed rice cake with a warm centre of coconut and jaggery. This guide adapts the Bangladeshi favourite for a kitchen in Japan, using ingredients you can look for locally.

Ingredients

The original recipe uses rice flour, coconut, jaggery and a pinch of salt. The quantities below are a practical home version; add liquid slowly because different rice flours absorb different amounts.

🌾

Rice flour

Look for 上新粉 (jōshinko), a non-glutinous rice flour. Texture may vary by brand.

2 cups (about 300 g)
🥥

Grated coconut

Unsweetened frozen shredded coconut, thawed. Fresh coconut also works.

1 cup (about 60 g)
🍯

Jaggery / palm sugar

Shave or finely chop the block so it melts more evenly. Okinawan 黒糖 (kokutō) is a substitute if needed.

About ½ cup
🧂

Salt + liquid

A pinch of salt and warm water to dampen the flour. Coconut milk is optional, not required.

Start with 150–200 ml
Important: The source recipe uses about 375 ml water for 525 g rice flour. This smaller version uses less flour, so do not pour in all the liquid at once. Stop when the flour resembles damp sand—not a smooth dough.

Step-by-step method

You’ll need a mixing bowl, a sieve, a small bowl or 8–10 cm mould, a pot or steamer, and clean heat-safe muslin or cheesecloth.

1

Moisten the rice flour

Put the jōshinko and a pinch of salt in a bowl. Sprinkle in warm water a little at a time, rubbing it through the flour with your fingertips.

Texture check: Squeeze a handful—it should hold together. Touch it and it should crumble back into grains. Do not knead it into bread-like dough.

2

Rest for 15–20 minutes

Cover the damp flour with a clean cloth or lid and leave it to rest so moisture spreads evenly through the grains.

3

Sieve until light and grainy

Push the damp mixture through a medium metal sieve with your fingers or the back of a spoon. Break up large lumps. This helps make the finished pitha lighter instead of dense.

4

Layer the filling

Set a small bowl or mould (about 8–10 cm wide) on a plate. Loosely fill it halfway with sieved rice flour. Add a little shaved jaggery and a layer of thawed grated coconut.

Cover with more rice flour until the filling is hidden. Do not pack it down tightly; steam needs to move through the grains.

5

Prepare the steamer safely

Bring water to a steady boil in a pot. Use a fitted metal steamer insert if you have one. For the cloth method, use clean, heat-safe cheesecloth secured tightly over the pot opening, well above the water. Keep the cloth and fastener away from the flame.

Safety: Do not use a loose cloth that could slip into the pot. Steam can cause serious burns.

6

Turn out the pitha

Place a small clean cloth over the filled mould, then carefully and quickly invert it onto the steamer surface. Lift the mould away gently so the mound stays on the cloth. If it falls apart, the flour may be too dry; lightly sprinkle in a little more water and sieve again.

7

Steam until set

Cover with a lid and steam for about 5–10 minutes. Check that the pitha holds its shape and feels set when lightly tapped. Larger or denser pithas may need longer; avoid relying on time alone.

8

Serve warm

Turn off the heat. Carefully lift the pitha with a spatula and peel away the cloth, keeping your hands clear of the steam. Eat while warm, when the coconut is fragrant and the jaggery is soft.

Shopping guide

Finding ingredients in Japan

Try larger supermarkets, international food shops, or Indian/Bangladeshi grocery stores. Check the package label rather than relying on a product’s appearance alone.

Best place to start

上新粉 — Jōshinko

Non-glutinous rice flour. It is a reasonable Japanese starting point, though the final texture may differ from freshly ground Bangladeshi rice flour.

Avoid for this texture

白玉粉 / もち粉

These are glutinous-rice flours used for chewy Japanese sweets. They can make the pitha much stickier and more mochi-like than intended.

Filling

冷凍ココナッツ

Choose unsweetened frozen shredded coconut where possible. Thaw it and drain away excess liquid before layering.

Sweetener

グラジャ / 黒糖

Jaggery from an Indian or Bangladeshi shop gives a more traditional flavour. Okinawan kokutō is a practical backup, but tastes different.

Troubleshooting

Three things that matter most

💧Keep it like damp sand

Too wet makes a paste; too dry makes the pitha crumble before steaming. Add liquid gradually and sieve after resting.

🌬️Do not compress the layers

Loose, sieved grains allow steam to pass through. Firmly packing the flour can make the cake dense.

♨️Handle steam carefully

Keep the pot stable, secure the cloth, use a lid where appropriate, and lift the cloth away from your face and hands.
